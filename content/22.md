## Introduction

Last month, we witnessed one of the biggest Crypto exchange till date, Bybit, where the hackers exploited the wallet's UI and stole $1.5 million worth of crypto.
Bybit, one of the largest cryptocurrency exchanges, recently fell victim to a sophisticated cyberattack resulting in the theft of approximately **400,000 ETH (worth $1.5 billion)**. This breach, attributed to the infamous **Lazarus Group**, underscores the evolving threats in the crypto space and the critical need for **robust security measures**. In this blog, we analyze how the hack occurred, its implications, and what security engineers can learn from it.

---

## How the Bybit Hack Happened

### 1. **How Bybit and Safe Work Together**

Bybit relied on **Safe{Wallet}**, a third-party multi-signature wallet service, to manage its cold and hot wallet infrastructure. Safe provides **smart contract-based wallet security**, ensuring transactions require multiple approvals before execution. Bybit used this system to sign transactions securely, but this reliance also introduced a vulnerability that was exploited in the attack.

### 2. **Involvement of Cold Wallets**

Unlike many past exchange hacks that targeted hot wallets, this attack extended to **Bybit’s cold wallet infrastructure**. Since cold wallets store large amounts of funds offline, they are considered more secure. However, the breach occurred because Bybit’s **security team unknowingly signed malicious transactions**, effectively moving assets from cold storage into hacker-controlled wallets.

### 3. **Exploiting a Third-Party Wallet Service**

The attack originated from a **compromise in Bybit’s multi-signature wallet system**, which relied on Safe. The attackers managed to infiltrate Safe’s infrastructure by **hacking into a Safe developer’s computer**.

### 4. **Malicious UI Injection and Improper Message Verification**

Once inside, the hackers **injected malicious JavaScript** into the **Safe frontend** used by Bybit’s security team. This attack vector enabled them to manipulate the transaction approval process.

A critical mistake that led to the breach was the **failure to properly check the messages being signed**. The attackers disguised their transactions to appear as legitimate fund transfers. Bybit’s team, relying on Safe’s frontend, signed these transactions without detecting the fraudulent intent, leading to the unauthorized movement of funds.

### 5. **Blockchain Laundering Techniques**

After successfully transferring the stolen ETH, the hackers employed sophisticated obfuscation techniques:

- **Bridging assets** across multiple blockchains to break traceability
- **Mixing services** to conceal ownership
- **Thousands of microtransactions** to fragment stolen assets

## Attribution: Who Was Behind It?

According to the **FBI and Chainalysis**, the attack was orchestrated by **North Korea’s Lazarus Group**. The group is notorious for targeting crypto exchanges to fund illicit activities, including weapons development. They quickly converted portions of the stolen ETH into **Bitcoin and privacy-focused cryptocurrencies** to evade tracking.

---

## Bybit’s Response

Despite the massive loss, Bybit swiftly took the following actions:

- **Emergency Reserve Funding**: Bybit replenished its reserves within **72 hours**, securing 447,000 ETH from firms like **Galaxy Digital, FalconX, and Wintermute**.
- **1:1 Asset Backing Assurance**: Bybit confirmed that **user funds remained unaffected**, as all assets were backed 1:1. This ensured that no users experienced financial loss despite the scale of the breach.
- **Bounty Offer**: A **$140 million bounty** was announced for information leading to the tracking and freezing of stolen assets.
- **Security Upgrades**: Strengthened multi-signature controls, revised third-party security policies, and implemented stricter **internal transaction approval mechanisms**.

## Lessons for Security Engineers

This hack highlights crucial security lessons for crypto exchanges and security engineers:

### 1. **Limit Third-Party Dependencies**

- Conduct deep security audits before integrating external wallet services.
- Ensure **strict endpoint monitoring** for supply-chain risks.

### 2. **Enhance Transaction Approval Processes**

- Implement **multi-factor authentication (MFA) for all critical transactions**.
- Use **hardware security modules (HSMs)** instead of relying solely on software-based approvals.

### 3. **Strengthen Insider Threat Detection**

- Continuously monitor **developer activities and access logs**.
- Deploy **behavioral analytics** to flag anomalies in transaction signing processes.

### 4. **Proactive Blockchain Forensics**

- Maintain **on-chain monitoring tools** to detect unusual fund movements in real-time.
- Establish partnerships with firms like **Chainalysis and TRM Labs** to track and recover stolen assets.
